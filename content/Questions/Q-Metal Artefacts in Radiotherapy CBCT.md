# Background
Patient is being treated for a pelvic region cancer, they have metal in this region and because of this have very large star artefacts which cover the whole patient.  At hull we use on-set [[CT#CBCT|CBCT]] every day to position the patient for their treatment, this is is conjunction with surface guided radiotherapy which does the initial positioning and gating.
Can they be treated?

# Initial thoughts
While the star artefact does worsen the quality of the image—especially around the metal itself—the majority of the patients boney structures, which are the most useful for matching, are fine.  There are no changes we can really make to the on-set imaging to reduce these artefacts which leaves us with 2(ish) options.
- Continue treatment as we are, could require senior radiographer to okay match on each day to ensure more experienced team-member has seen it.
- Re-plan using larger tolerances.  Would require discussion with clinician as to what they would feel is appropriate?
I'd lean towards option, as increasing the tolerances will have clinical impact on the plan, although it could be worth comparing to the initial one to see  how large this impact is.  
If we have scan procedures (increased projections etc) that can reduce these artefacts these could also be worth considering, however the dose increase would likely require clinician approval.

# Post-Discussion
Key important pieces of information
- Not visible on day 1
- On left day 2
- On right and inferior day 3
- ~7000 HU

From the fact that the artefact has been changing place compared to previous imaging it is clearly within the bowel and moving, with 7000 being roughly in-line with a dental filling.  Upon asking the patient they had swallowed one of their fillings the week before.  This should have minor dosimetric impact on the patient's treatment—especially as it is moving between fractions—however it may cause issues in later fractions if it becomes proximal to the PTV.
