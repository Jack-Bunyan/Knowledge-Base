Technetium is by far the most routinely used radio-isotope in nuclear medicine.  It's metastable state TC-99m is produced by the beta decay of molybdenum 99 in a [[Technetium-99m Generator|Generator]].  This beta decay results in the excited technetium state 88% of the time
![[Pasted image 20260729151129.png]]
[Decay process](http://www.hyperphysics.phy-astr.gsu.edu/hbase/Nuclear/technetium.html) for the metastable technetium isotope.

Technetium has a half life of 6hrs and releases a gamma ray with an energy of 140.5keV, this is useful as the half life is short enough that the patient will not be radioactive for long and any contamination poses little long term risk; but, also long enough that the scan process is not rushed.

The energy of the photon is high enough that it will not be heavily attenuated by the patient, increasing the signal that is produced from a radioactive tracer given to the patient.  The high energy of the photon also increases the number of photons produced within the scintillator crystal, improving anger logic precision and spatial resolution.

## Tc99m-DTPA
![[DTPA.png]]
DTPA is a molecule that is used for kidney and lung scans, the molecules are quite large, this means that they get stuck in the [[Anatomy & Physiology-Kidneys|kidney]], specifically filtered via the glomeruli with no tubular excretion.  The molecules are used for a range of scans such as blood flow, [[GFR]] measurement, and [[Lung VQ|Lung ventilation imaging]].
