The heart is located near the centre of the body slightly towards the left hand side, reducing the size of the left lung.  The apex points toward the left hip, while the base points towards the right should (tilted as shown in below image).  

- The general principles of cardiac function are:
1. The right side of the heart pumps deoxygenated (venous) blood into the heart.
2. The left side of the heart pumps oxygenated (arterial) blood away from the heart.
3. The vessels carrying blood to the heart are called veins.
4. The vessels carrying blood away from the heart are called arteries.



![[Diagram_ofHeart_chambers.png]]
- The heart consists of four chambers; a right and left atrium and a right and left ventricle.
	- The right atrium contains the SA node pacemaker, which ensures that there is regular muscle motion
	- The Ventricles of the heart operate at very different pressures:
		- Left: 120/80mmHg
		- Right: 25/0-5mmHg
	- The left ventricle has a significantly thicker wall, at 8-12mm
- The left and right chambers are not directly connected and are split by a wall, the septum.
- Separating the atria, ventricles and great vessels are four valves that allow unidirectional circulation of blood.
    - These valves open and close as a result of changes in blood pressure.
    - The Atrioventricular valves open during diastole and close during systole, with the role of preventing retrograde flow to the atria
    - The semilunar valves open during systole and close during diastole, preventing arterial backflow

The walls of the heart are a vital aspects of its working, constructed from striated [[Muscle#Cardiac|Cardiac Muscle]], this is involuntary muscle which continuously pumps [[blood]] around the body.  This can be measured using various imaging techniques such as [[Ultrasound]] or [[Radionuclide Ventriculography|RNVG nuclear medicine]] scans.  In order to ensure that this process is continuous the muscle of the heart has an incredibly large amount of [[Cells#Mitochondria|Mitochondria]], with around 30% of the cell volume being taken up by them.

This also makes it important that the blood flow to the heart is maintained, with the coronary vessels maintaining this.

# Imaging

[[Ultrasound]] can be used to measure both the function and the structure of the heart, performing measurements of the thickness of the ventricle walls, as well as using doppler imaging to measure flow rate around the heart.  Clinical indications such as the the stroke volume or ejection fraction (measured using [[Radionuclide Ventriculography]]) can be used to indicate heart function.

Other measurements of the cardiac function such as the use of [[Thallium Redistribution|Perfusion Imaging]] which show the function of the coronary arteries are used to measure the viability of heart muscle.

Understanding the function of the heart is important for many areas of imaging, if wanting accurate anatomical images of the heart it is vital to gate the images to maintain spatial resolution.
