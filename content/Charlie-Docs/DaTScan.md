>This primarily scan looks at differential diagnosis of Parkinson’s Disease through uptake in caudate and putamen.

[Current Guidance](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng71/chapter/Recommendations) Advises the use of DaT scans for cases where patients have a tremor that cannot be classified as essential or parkinsonism tremor.

- DRL range was to support flexibility in patient dose without constant [[IRMER#Practiotioner|Arsac Holder]] involvement when patients arrived late
- [[Iodine#I-123 Scans|I-123]] Ioflupane is a cocaine derivative that binds to dopamine transporters (DAT) and blockades them
- preventing any movement of dopamine across the [[Nervous System#Nerve Cells|Synapse]].
- The radioligand (ioflupane) will only bind to functioning DATs in dopaminergic axons.

>This means function of the caudate and putamen can be interpreted through count intensity. this also allows for quantification of scans (DATQUANT).

- Uptake in the brain is rapid, ~7% of injected activity after 10 minutes and 3% of activity after 5 hours.
	- ~ 30% of brain uptake is attributed to striatal uptake.
![[synapse Diagram for DATScan.png]]
- Labelled ioflupane blocks the dopamine transporter on the presynaptic neuron.
- Once these have joined to the dopamine receptor, they are bound and will block any redistribution.
- There are around 450,000 dopamine neurons in the brain.

1) Injection and Distribution: I-123 ioflupane is administered intravenously. Once in the bloodstream, it circulates throughout the body, including the [[brain]].
2) [[brain#Blood-Brain Barrer|Blood Brain Barrier]] Penetration: The compound must cross the blood-brain barrier (BBB) to reach brain tissue. Ioflupane is lipophilic (fat-soluble), which facilitates its passage through the BBB.
3) Binding to Dopamine Transporters: In the brain, I-123 ioflupane specifically targets and binds to dopamine transporters (DaT) located on the presynaptic membranes of dopaminergic neurons. These transporters are primarily found in the striatum, a region of the brain involved in movement control.
4) Accumulation in the Striatum: Because of its high affinity for dopamine transporters, I-123 ioflupane accumulates in the striatum. This selective binding is what allows for the visualization of dopaminergic neuron density.
5) Imaging with [[Nuclear medicine#SPECT|SPECT]]: Single Photon Emission Computed Tomography (SPECT) is used to detect the gamma radiation emitted by the I-123 isotope. The SPECT camera captures images of the distribution of I-123 ioflupane in the brain, highlighting areas with high concentrations of dopamine transporters.
6) Image Interpretation: The resulting images show the relative concentration of dopamine transporters in different regions of the brain. In conditions such as Parkinson’s disease, there is a significant reduction in dopamine transporter levels in the striatum, which can be visualised as reduced radiotracer uptake in these areas.

# Anatomy and Physiology
![[Saggital Image of Brain DaTScan.png]]

- Within the centre of the brain, there are a group of structures that are thought to be responsible for motor learning, emotional behaviours and habit formation.
	- this is known as the basal ganglia
- Disruption of this network form the idea for several movement disorders such as Parkinson’s disease.
- It is thought to operate through several loops (or circuits) allowing for signal transfer through a complex network ([more information on this](https://www.physio-pedia.com/Basal_Ganglia)).

# Patient Preparation

- [[Thyroid]] Blockade: Typically 130mg potassium iodide tablets (usually 2 x 65mg tablets, equivalent to a total of 100mg of iodide)) to be taken at least 1 hour before the injection
    - If potassium iodide is unavailable, alternatives such as Lugol's solution can be used (0.8ml contains 104mg of iodide). Can be given with milk or water/cordial.
    - See the ARSAC Notes for Guidance (section 8) for further details and alternatives
- Remove hearing aids, ear-rings, hair clips etc
- Patients do not need to stop any medications prior to DaTScan (based on discussions with Prof Avery & Southampton University Hospitals) but should bring with them a list of their current medications.
    - This is currently under review with respect to the use of quantification. This protocol will be reissued with further instructions shortly. See comments at the end of the document

# Normal Results - new pics from xeleris

Caudate and putamen show focal uptake - creating an accentuated comma.

Any unilateral uptake is generally abnormal.

Noisy image with little contrast from background to caudate/putamen is considered abnormal.

# Referral Criteria Explanation
![[DaTScan referral criteria.png]]
- Differentiation between Essential Tremor/ Drug Induced PD, and Parkinsonians syndromes
- Assessment of the severity and progression of Parkinsonian syndomres
- Differentiation between Alzheimer’s Disease and Dementia with Lewy Bodies/Parkinsonian Dementia


# Extras

- Check if patient is allergic to iodine by checking if they’re allergic to seafood or milk.
    - Many types of seafood contain high levels of iodine.
- Amoxapine, amphetamine, benztropine, bupropion, buspirone, cocaine, mazindol, methamphetamine, methylphenidate, norephedrine, phentermine, phenylpropanolamine, selegiline, sertraline, citalopram and paroxetine may interfere with DaTscan imaging.

# Imaging Procedure

- Place bed extension on camera bed so the shoulders are just over the edge of the bed.
- Wrap the sponges in couch roll.
    - These are used to reduce head movement and placed either side of the patient’s head in the holder.
- Place a folded couch roll in head rest.
- Strap patient’s head up (folded couch roll needed at CHH, not at HRI).
- Press set, stop movement when bed is ~70cm.
    - This height isn’t mandatory, but this allows majority of the detector movement to be done simultaneously.
- Move patient in so the eyes are within the FOV:
    - Due to location of caudate and putamen, having the eyes in FOV will always ensure that they are within the scan and not being clipped (see image below).
![[DaTScan coloiurful.png]]
 - Bring both detectors in slowly getting as close as possible on anterior/posterior.
- Move detectors individually so radius for both detectors are identical.
- Slowly rotate detectors and make sure detectors aren’t touching anywhere.
