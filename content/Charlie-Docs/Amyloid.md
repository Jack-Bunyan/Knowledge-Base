>This scan looks at [amyloidosis](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/amyloidosis/) of the [[Anatomy & Physiology-Heart|heart]].

Current guidance from the [European Society of Cardiology](https://www.escardio.org/communities/councils/cardiology-practice/scientific-documents-and-publications/ejournal/volume-19/cardiac-amyloidosis-epidemiology-diagnosis-and-therapy/) suggests the use of [[Nuclear medicine]] imaging if biochemical testing is not successful.

Locally we use DPD for this, though PYP is another popular radio-pharmaceutical that sees use.
![[PYP and DPD.png]]

# Pharmaceutical, Uptake, and Dose

| Pharmaceutical              | [[Tc99m]] DPD (Teceos) |
| --------------------------- | ---------------------- |
| ARSAC DRL                   | N/A (unlicensed)       |
| Local DRL                   | 800MBq                 |
| Paediatric Minimum Activity | N/A                    |
| Uptake Time                 | 3 hours                |
| Collimators                 | LEHRS                  |
- The radio-pharmaceutical is [[bone]]-seeking agent that can also bind to amyloid fibrils within heart tissue.
	- The uptake mechanism is not fully understood, but it is believed it has to do [[calcium]]-mediated binding.
>Amyloid fibrils are insoluble [[proteins]] that are immune to degradation, these are formed from normal protein chains that fold incorrectly

Tc99m DPD (technetium-99m 3,3-diphosphono-1,2-propanodicarboxylic acid) is a bone-seeking radiopharmaceutical that can also bind to amyloid fibrils in the heart. The exact mechanism of uptake in cardiac amyloidosis is not fully understood, but it is thought to involve calcium-mediated binding to amyloid fibrils.


# Patient Preparation
1. When the patient attends, ask them for their completed questionnaire ([AF062](https://wiki.heynm.org.uk/doku.php?id=forms:af062-bone_scan_patient_questionnaire)). Give this to a member of admin staff for scanning into RIS.
2. For inpatients, ask the patient if they have any surgery booked for the same day. If yes, discuss with one of the nuclear medicine physicists prior to injecting the patient.
3. Ask the patient to empty their [[bladder]] just before imaging.
    - Incontinent patients must change to a clean pad and catheter bags must be emptied before the images.
4. Ask the patient to remove any metallic objects such as keys, coins, chains and belts prior to imaging. If metallic articles on clothing cannot be removed, ask the patient to wear a gown during imaging. Alternatively, if the effect on the images is small, the location and size should be noted in the attendance comments field in the Radiology Information System (RIS). If the patient has metallic implants such as a pacemaker or prosthetic joint, the location and date of surgery should be noted in the attendance comments field in RIS.
5. If the patient has a breast prosthesis, ask them to remove it.

# Procedure
The procedure involves:

1. Intravenous injection of Tc99m DPD
2. 3-hour uptake period
3. Whole-body planar imaging
4. Additional SPECT/CT imaging of the chest if necessary

The scan is used to detect and assess the extent of cardiac involvement in transthyretin (ATTR) amyloidosis, which can be either hereditary or wild-type (senile systemic amyloidosis).

Images are caputred using LEHRS [[collimator]] and an energy window of $140(.5)keV\pm10\%$, scatter window on discovery of $120keV\pm5\%$. 
# Normal Results
- We acquire a normal [[whole body bone]] scan (DPD is a bone agent so we get our money’s worth), with [[SPECT]]/CT of chest.
- SPECT/CT is still completed even if heart uptake is absent in scan.
- Some centres only SPECT/CT if heart uptake present.

# Perugini Score
- This is the accepted scale for evaluating the extent of amyloidosis of the heart:
- Grade 0 - no cardiac uptake and normal rib uptake
- Grade 1 - cardiac uptake which is less than rib uptake
- Grade 2 - cardiac uptake with intensity similar to rib uptake
- Grade 3 - cardiac uptake greater than rib uptake with mild or absent rib uptake
