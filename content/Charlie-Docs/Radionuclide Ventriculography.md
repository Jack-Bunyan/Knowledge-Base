- The pharmaceutical used for this scan is 99mTc-Pertechnetate.
    - this is the eluate from the generator
        - more info found on this [**Technetium-99m Generator**](https://app.notion.com/p/Technetium-99m-Generator-bab4fff292ae486192d4c30570955c46?pvs=21).
![[pertechnetate molecule image.png]]
*Chemical structure of 99mTc-Pertechnetate, an anion, in a tetrahedral structure.*

- Upon patient’s arrival, they are injected with stannous pyrophosphate (PYP).
    - this loads the [[On Blood#Red Blood Cells|Red Blood Cells]], through _“stannous loading”_, and allows the radioactive tracer to bind to it, thus marking red blood cells.
    - without the injection of PYP, the pertechnetate would freely diffuse in and out of erythrocytes. However, as the cells have been preloaded with the stannous ion, the [sodium pertechnetate becomes reduced](https://humanhealth.iaea.org/HHW/Radiopharmacy/VirRad/Radiolabelling_with_99mTc/chemical_processes/index.html) within the cells and becomes bound to the globin.
    - this binding processes/mechanisms of the sodium pertechnetate to the tin loaded erythrocytes is not clearly understood.  *My understanding is that the reduced pertechnetate cannot diffuse through the cell membrane as easily as the non-reduced form.*
- Injected with pertechnetate after 30 minute uptake time of PYP, imaging can begin immediately.
    - around 20% of pertechnetate is believed to be bound to a beta chain of globin.

# Anatomy and Physiology

---

- See [[Anatomy & Physiology-Heart]].
- Every heartbeat, the left ventricle contracts causing oxygenated blood to be pushed around the body.
    - the amount of blood (%) that can be pushed out of the left ventricle is known as the left ventricle ejection fraction (LVEF).
    - it is calculated in this test with the following formula:

> 	$LVEF(\%) = ((diastolic Counts - systolicCounts)/diastolicCounts) *100$

# Patient Preparation

---

- No specific patient preparation is required.
- Although these tests must not be repeated within 3 months of one another.
    - this is due to the long-lasting fixation of stannous salts on red blood cells.
        - want all of the stannous salts removed from blood cells to give an accurate representation of ejection fraction.

## PYP Preparation

- Weigh the patient to determine the volume of PYP to be administered to the patient.
    
- Clean the top of the PYP vial and saline bottle with a Helapet wipe.
    
    - use a different side of the Helapet for the vial and saline.
- Using the blunt needle, 10ml syringe and saline ampoule, draw up 6ml of saline.
    
- Remove the blunt needle and place back in the red guard.
    
- Now attach a safety needle onto the 10ml syringe and put 6ml of saline into the PYP vial and lightly shake to mix.
    
    - make sure to remove 6ml of air afterwards, so the pressure in the vial stays stable.
    - close the guard on the safety needle to avoid stabbing yourself.
- Open the 5ml syringe and attach the other safety needle.
    
- Draw up the required dose into the syringe, flick the syringe to remove any air bubbles.
    
    - remove the safety needle and close the guard.
- The PYP is now ready to administer, after second checking, of course.
    
    ### Equipment Needed
    
    - 1x 10ml Syringe
    - 1x 5ml Syringe
    - 1x Blunt Needle
    - 2x Safety Needle
    - 1x Helapet Wipe
    - 1x 10ml Saline Ampoule
    - 1x PYP Vial (in fridge)

## What shows up?

> An ejection fraction (EF) < 40% is considered abnormal. The higher the percentage, the greater the amount of blood that is pushed out of the left ventricle.

## Extras

- In-vitro labelling of red blood cells (enthrocytes) has ~60% efficiency, hence the high dose.
    - Previously, this scan required a QA image of the thyroid as this is where the free pertechnetate was taken up.
- Scan is gated, 32 frames/cycle.
    - Each PQRST wave is divided up into 32 sections and an image is acquired for each of these frames.
- This can also be done as a hybrid “first-pass” study.
    - Doctors in the past have said it is easier to see separation when positioning.
