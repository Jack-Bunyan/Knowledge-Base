- 99mTc-_HSA_ nanosized colloid (Nanoscan) is a filtered colloid solution.
    - HSA = human serum albumin, the most abundant protein in [[On Blood#Plasma|Plasma]]
    - has an average particle diameter of 80nm
- After subcutaneous (intradermal) injection, 30-40% of the administered colloid is filtered into lymphatic capillaries to be transported along the lymphatic vessels.
- Upon arrival in the lymph nodes, the colloid is trapped in the reticular cells of functioning nodes.
## Anatomy and Physiology

![[Node Image.png]]
- The lymphatic system is responsible for moving lymph fluid.
	- lymph fluid contains; lipids, proteins, enzymes, urea, minerals, hormones, dissolved gasses, cells (macrophages and lymphocytes), toxins, bacteria, viruses and various cell debris.
- Lymph fluid travels freely between cells all around the body.
	- it does this to further filter extracellular fluid
	- the fluid ‘leaks’ from blood vessels
- The lymph nodes are responsible for transferring this fluid back into the bloodstream for excretion via the kidneys.

# Patient Preparation

---

- Ask the patient when their surgery is - if not same or next day then don’t do the injection

# Normal Scan Results
---
![[NM image of nodes.png]]
- Injection site should be imaged, lymphatic vessels may be seen.
- Sentinel lymph node is the first lymph node that the cancer cells would be most likely to spread to.
- Multiple nodes may be visualised, varying by individuals.
- Should be ensured that the injection does not go into the blood, as this would pollute measurements

The patient will then head to surgery.  A gamma probe is used to identify the locations where the extracellular injection has aggregated within the nodes, allowing them to only remove nodes where the tumour would spread to immediately.  These nodes are removed and studied for markers of cancer, which would indicate non local disease.

### Sentinel Node Localisation
---
- This has become the standard in care in [breast cancer](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng101/chapter/Recommendations) and [malignant melanoma management](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng14/chapter/Recommendations#staging-with-sentinel-lymph-node-biopsy)
- An injection of Nanocolloid is given in close proximity to the primary neoplasm to determine the lymph node(s) draining the site and possibly [[Oncology#metastasis|metastisizing]] tumour cells.
	- It should not be injected into the tumour as this could delay lymphatic drainage for hours.
	- This is a result of the higher density of cells within tumours
- Should there be no SLN, the site should be gently massaged with heat to encourage lymphatic drainage.
	- In ~1-2% of patients, there will be no SLN identification
		- The factors causing this may be: old age, obesity, pre-op wire insertion or replacement of SLN by the tumour.
- Depending on the injection site, there are different pathways of drainage:
	- Breast - axillary lymph nodes (expected) but can drain to internal mammary, supraclavicular, intercostal and chest wall lymph nodes.
		- Internal lymph nodes are not biopsied as they are difficult to access
	- Trunk - axillary lymph nodes (~90% of time) but some back lesions will drain to multiple sites or to the contralateral.
- Once the patient is in surgery, it is likely that multiple lymph nodes have accumulated nanocolloid.
	- Many surgeons combine gamma probe counts and information obtained using blue dye to decrease false-negatives and increase the sensitivity of the test.
	- This will also aid in the ease of procedure as it reduces the nead for the gamma probe once the surgery has begun

# New Methods
---
There is [current research](https://www.nice.org.uk/consultations/1461/1/the-technology) looking into the use of magnetic tracers (magtrace and sentimag), there is currently [consultation](https://www.bmj.com/content/377/bmj.o1226) looking into replacing conventional radioactive node tracers with these instead.  This would reduce the radioactive dose of the patient and potentially be cheaper; studies showing that while requiring slightly more skill than gamma probe localisation this technology does have similar(or slightly better) accuracy.
