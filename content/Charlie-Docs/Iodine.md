Iodine plays an essential role within human physiology, used for the synthesis of tetraiodothyronine ($T_4$) and triiodothyroine ($T_3$).  These hormones are important for thermal regulation, increasing the body metabolism and boosting thermogenesis.

Radioiodine uptake can be seen in [[thyroid]], [[salivary glands]], [[stomach]] and choroid plexus. Excretion is via [[Anatomy & Physiology-Kidneys|kidneys]] and [[gastrointestinal tract]] so these will be seen in images.

- Uptake is rapid in the thyroid, usually in 20-30 minutes and continues to increase over 24 hours.

# I-131 Therapy

>This therapy is used to ablate remnant, local/distant thyroid metastases.

![[I-131 Decay.png]]

Iodine-131 is a radio-isotope which decays via [[Radioactive Decay#Beta Decay|Beta Decay]] into Xe-131, it has a half-life of 8 days and releases both an electron with a maximum energy of 606keV and a gamma ray of 364keV.

## NICE Guidance
I-131 treatment is recommended for [[Thyroid]] cases where the patient has already received a complete thyroidectomy, and is not recommended in cases  where the tumour has not progressed past T1a/b

The default activity is assumed to be 1.1GBq, however in cases where the patient shows:
- T3, N1b, M1
- Multiple ablations should be avoided
3.7GBq is also reccomended.

## Dose

The responsible [[IRMER#Practitioner|Consultant]] for the patient decides on the dose for the capsule based on extent of the disease as well as the clinical history of the patient. Repeat ‘offenders’ may recieve higher doses such as 5.5GBq.

On the morning before administration, the capsule is assayed and checked by two [[IRMER#Operator|Physicists]], ensuring the dose is suitable for the patient.  This is done using a [[Calibrators/Monitors#Calibrator|Calibrator]]. 

## Patient Preparation

- If not undergoing thyrogen (rhTSH) stimulation:
- Stop Levothyroxine (T4) for a minimum of 4 weeks prior to the therapy.
- Stop Lyothyronine (T3) for a minimum of 2 weeks prior to the therapy.
- If undergoing thyrogen (rhTSH) stimulation:
- Intramuscular thyrogen (rhTSH) injections on the two days prior to the therapy
- If patients are to be treated straight after surgery without hormone replacement, a minimum of three weeks should be allowed after surgery before the 131I treatment
- Avoid iodine medications containing iodine (e.g. antiseptics, eye drops, iodinated multivitamins or mineral supplements) for a minimum of 2 weeks
- 8 weeks after administration of [[Iodine#Contrast|Iodinated contrast Media]] (as used in CT and X-ray procedures)
- Amiodarone may have to be withdrawn for 12 months to ensure optimal conditions for 131I ablation or therapy. For patients taking Amiodarone, discuss this with the referrer

## **Diet**

- Follow a [Low-iodine diet](http://www.heynm.org.uk/patientinformation/I004-i131_low_iodine_diet.html) for a minimum of 2 weeks prior to the treatment
- Fast for a minimum of 4 hours prior to the treatment. **NB:** Diabetic patients may eat a light meal

## **Other**

- Discontinue breast feeding for a minimum of 8 weeks prior to the therapy, not to recommence until any future pregnancy
- Blood tests for TSH and Thyroglobulin on the day of the therapy, prior to the administration of the 131I

## Procedure

Bloods are taken by a doctor whilst patients are on the ward, immediately before receiving the capsule as well as sats being checked by a nurse. Whilst this is being completed, QC of the Fluke and CoMo are performed as these will be used after administration. The room is checked it has drinks, snacks, towels, the bed is made and everything is packexed/wrapped in cling film (e.g. remotes).

Alike [[Radium#Ra-223 Therapy|Ra-223 Therapy]], the patients are talked through a questionnaire - asking questions about home life and local rules whilst staying in the iodine suite, as well as prerequisites to receiving the capsule;

- House Drainage
- Sexual Contact
- Cooking
- Childcare
- Sleeping Arrangements
- Advice on personal belongings brought along

In cases where there are personal situations that may impact the ability for these to be carried out a risk assessment must be carried out in order to evaluate the potential harm and inform the clinicians treatment decision.

![[I131 checklist p1.png|329]]![[I131 checklist p2.png|330]]

Upon the room containing all of the required items and all of the appropriate checks being completed, the patient signs a consent form included in their management booklet to consent to the treatment for the final time. The capsule is administered without touching the capsule due to the high dose and potential contamination risk. After swallowing the capsule, the patient is left alone in the suite and is required to continue fasting for the next hour. The patient may eat again once the baseline dose rate meter reading (by the Fluke) has been taken. This is performed by standing one meter away from the patient and holding the dose rate meter in line with the patient’s belly button, taking an anterior and posterior measurement. This dose rate is assumed to be 100% of the capsules radioactivity and will be used on discharge day when calculating remaining activity in the patient. Generally after 3-5 days, a second measurement is taken to see if the patient is available to leave the iodine suite. As long as the dose is <800MBq then the patient is fine to leave, as long as they still follow the precautions outlined above.

## Extras

Ablation to kill off any thyroid cancer cells before they have chance to differentiate (once the cancer differentiates, it is no longer treatable as thyroid cancer and has progressed into secondary cancer that is a lot more difficult to treat).

## Post-treatment Imaging

Due to the NICE guidance surrounding how the initial imaging is performed it is likely that prior to the I-131 ablation they have **not** received a nuclear medicine scan.  Whilst the purpose of the I-131 is the beta particles that ablate the local thyroid cancer, it also has gamma emissions.

Specifically when the I-131 beta decays it transitions to a Xe-131 atom, however, one of these is an excited state that decays near immediately with a 364keV.  After the therapy imaging is conducted using a [[Gamma Cameras, Classical and Solid State|Gamma Camera]] and high energy [[Collimator]].

# I-123 Scans
![[I-123 Decau.png]]

Iodine-123 decays via [[Radioactive Decay#Electron Capture|Electron Capture]] with a gamma ray of 159keV and a half-life of 13.2 hours.

[Nice Guidance](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng230/chapter/Recommendations) advises against performing these scans as routine for thyroid cancer.

We have thyroid scans to look for thyrotoxicosis, nuc med scan after ultrasound scan. Nuc med scan looks at function of thyroid nodules and how active the thyroid is. Uptake of thyroid vs activity in syringe can give a quantitative result for the thyroid function. Thyroiditis caused from certain heart medication, side effect, not done as much anymore. If patient is still on thyroxin, no counts under the camera as hormone in thyroxin makes thyroid not avid for pertechnetate.

I-123 can be used for scans of both the thyroid and the parathyroid.  It relies on the same pharmokinetics as ablation therapy, so the patient preperation will be the same.

## Imaging Procedure
Static images are taken using both planar and [[Nuclear medicine#SPECT|SPECT]] images.  The planar images are taken with a pinhole [[Collimator]], while a parallel hole LEHR is used for the SPECT.

An energy window of $159keV \pm 10\%$ is used.
